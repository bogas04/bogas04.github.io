# Editorial review

The author's voice and flow take priority over conventional polish. A review is
not a rewrite.

## Changes an agent may make directly

- Fix obvious typos, missing words, missing commas, and clear grammar mistakes.
- Preserve deliberate informal phrasing, cadence, word choice, and variants such
  as `dead end` or `deadend`. Do not standardise them merely for style.
- Preserve semicolons that explain or extend the preceding part of a sentence.
  Do not replace them simply to make the prose scan faster.
- Preserve sentences that are intentionally long or clunky. The author may want
  the reader to slow down, pay attention, and reread.
- Do not introduce em dashes or hyphens in blog prose.
- Do not introduce telltale AI phrasing, including `not just X but Y`, `and the
  worst part`, or definitive sentences built from several contrasting phrases.

## Changes an agent must suggest, not make

- Reordering, shortening, or rewriting passages for style or readability.
- Changes that alter the writer's stance, emotional intensity, humour, or
  distinctive phrasing.
- Anything uncertain enough that it might be intentional.

## Harm and sensitivity review

Flag published prose for the author's review if it makes broad claims about a
group or could be bigoted, racist, sexist, classist, ageist, or otherwise
insensitive. This is a request for review, not an instruction to silently
sanitise the author's perspective.

Inflammatory criticism may be intentional when it punches up at powerful people,
political parties, or corporations. Still flag wording that generalises about
people or a protected group. Keep the distinction between criticism of power and
harmful generalisation clear.
