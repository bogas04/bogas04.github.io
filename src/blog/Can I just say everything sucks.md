I was talking to my peers, and every single one of them currently feels anxious, uncertain and insecure about the future. Some are impacted by a [layoff](https://www.trueup.io/layoffs), some are anticipating one, some took a pay cut, while others are figuring out ways to make money to fund their life or parenthood. My nephew himself is concerned about which career to pick, as white-collar jobs seem to be dead ends or easily replaceable. I mean, what sort of world have we created that education is being undervalued? Is this made up? Is my internet wrong? Is it just my threatened privilege feeling concerned about its general expectation of "life will get better" not adding up anymore?

## suppose

Say you are indeed laid off today, not because you underperformed, but because a clanker has been trained by learning (read stealing) from millions of people like you who involuntarily built the knowledge base for it. What do you do now?

## jester-ification of education

Perhaps you become a creator. You package a skill, an opinion, a hobby, or a private moment into something that can hold attention long enough to be monetised.

To be clear, I don’t think content creation is lesser work, nor do I think social skill and analytical thinking are opposites. I’m unsettled by the possibility that more and more people may have to turn themselves into a product to survive. Attention becomes job security; personality becomes inventory.

The platforms reward urgency, spectacle, parasocial intimacy, and persuasion. Even people who begin by making useful, thoughtful work can be nudged towards constant performance and conversion. The fear is not that people make art, teach online, or build communities. It is that precarious work might push everyone into the same competition: perform, persuade, monetise, and keep performing or disappear.

I call this jesterification of education: not because creators are jesters, but because a system that promised stability through learning may now ask people to entertain the market to remain economically visible.

I don't know what the end game here is if all desk jobs are to be replaced with an "agent". If white-collar workers are not paid, who pays those who depend on the income of white-collar workers? It's late stage capitalism baby.

## systemic failure

Much of my angst is also coming from feeling abandoned due to the failure of democracy and corporations in providing law, order, stability and jobs. I mean, I come from a country that proclaims to be the mother of democracy, while actively curbing dissent. It is almost like it treats its own citizens as [_cockroaches_](https://www.thecockroachjantaparty.org.in/).

You can't walk for more than 100 meters in any city of this country no, really, any city—without seeing garbage, smelling the pollution, hearing excessive honking, playing the game of Subway Surfers IRL while walking on a nonexistent footpath, or feeling the free rollercoaster ride one gets by driving over potholes on frequently dug-up roads, all while sensing a lack of empathy for fellow citizens.

## scarcity

On empathy, I see a deep sense of scarcity in many people around me, especially in urban India. Why shouldn't they? Many have experienced generational poverty or insecurity at some point, and even if their parents had what would seem like a relatively rich life by today's standards, a paid-off house in their 30s, a cute hatchback car, two or more kids, and a long career at the same company ahead of them, with a pension, which they reluctantly had to retire from—there was, and still is, scarcity behind it all. One may even argue that this very scarcity mindset prevented lifestyle inflation back then.

That can make us see every person as a target to outpace, a seat in public transport to be "stolen" from a fellow passenger, and an inch of gap in traffic as a campsite until the next inch opens up. Of course, we feel entitled to these things because our problems were much worse, and now the world has to pay back by adjusting for our needs. We are always in a rush, never pausing to reflect on whether we are trading today's peace for a hypothetical peace in the future. Can anyone convince me that tomorrow will surely be better than today? If not, then why should I waste today in anxiety?

On one hand, we have more food than ever, more clothes than ever, and more money in the hands of very few than ever. Yet it feels like there is immense scarcity amid an abundance that remains unreachable. With education failing to provide employment, are we really carrying this scarcity mindset into much bleaker future? I shudder to imagine what that world would look like.

## get out

I am not the only one to rant online about systemic failures. It isn't hard to find comments like "Leave this country ASAP" or "Don't return back you'll regret it" online. Maybe these woes are indeed solved elsewhere. It isn't hard to find citizens of my country being surprised by how easily they are able to walk over 20K steps abroad, or how traffic could clear without a single honk. But then, it's not like the citizens of the countries my fellow citizens are fleeing to have a very positive outlook on life themselves, regardless of their political leanings. Housing crises or cost-of-living crises are just terms now; they don't even feel like "crises" that require "urgent" solutions.

It feels so strange that people feel they literally have to uproot their lives just to have a more humane experience.

## wait, was Karl right?

When I read about [Karl Marx's concept of alienation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marx%27s_theory_of_alienation), it looks like I'm simply describing symptoms of alienation. Even if he saw it coming, what have we done about it? What are we doing about it?

I'll ask again, is my internet wrong? Can you convince me that this is all in my head and life is actually beautiful? I am just feeling the loss of community and third spaces, because the point of life is not avoiding pain, but experiencing it with others. Whom am I asking this of? Are you listening? Do you feel this too?
